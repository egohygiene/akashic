## Orient First

### Evidence-state vocabulary

| State | Use it when | Do not turn it into |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Documented fact | Authenticated records, direct observation, or independent evidence establish the narrow proposition. | A license to attach unrelated conclusions. |
| Allegation | A person or institution has made a checkable claim that has not been established. | A fact merely because it is repeated or appears in an official filing. |
| Interpretation | The source explains known facts through an inference, model, or theory. | The underlying facts or the only possible explanation. |
| Satire or fiction | Context shows that a work is intentionally nonliteral, performative, or invented. | Evidence of a real event without independent corroboration. |
| Misinformation | Content is false or misleading regardless of why it was shared. | Proof that the sharer intended harm. |
| Disinformation | Evidence establishes deliberate deception, not merely error or disagreement. | A label inferred from falsity alone. |
| Unresolved | Material evidence is incomplete, inaccessible, contradictory, or genuinely contested. | Proof that every explanation is equally likely. |
| Falsified | A sufficiently specific claim conflicts with reliable evidence or failed a fair test. | Proof that every adjacent concern is false. |

**Uncertainty** describes what is not known and how that affects confidence. **Provenance** records where material came from and what happened to it. **Corroboration** is independent support for the same proposition. **Harm** includes risks to people, privacy, due process, health, and public safety. **Amplification** is the extra reach, legitimacy, or search visibility created by repeating a claim—even to criticize it.

### A minimum viable claim map

1. Write one narrow, falsifiable claim in neutral language; separate it from motive, implication, and identity.
2. Record the earliest source you can find, the date, the exact quotation or media segment, and the surrounding context.
3. Ask what evidence would distinguish the leading explanations and what result would change your confidence.
4. Prefer authenticated primary records, then seek genuinely independent corroboration and qualified analysis.
5. Track missing records, redactions, conflicts, corrections, incentives, and alternative explanations without treating absence as proof.
6. Assign an evidence state and confidence level; use “unresolved” when the record does not support a stronger conclusion.
7. Before publishing or sharing, minimize names and personal data, assess foreseeable harm, and ask whether repetition adds evidence or only amplification.

### Boundaries for difficult material

- A declassified document establishes that the document exists and says what it says; authenticity does not make every statement inside accurate or complete.
- An indictment, complaint, hearing, or investigative report records allegations, evidence, findings, or testimony according to its own legal status; it is not automatically a final adjudication.
- A fact-check is a transparent analysis to inspect, not an infallible authority. Read its sources, methods, corrections, scope, and funding disclosures.
- Metadata and Content Credentials can support provenance, but missing credentials do not prove fabrication and valid credentials do not prove that depicted events were interpreted correctly.
- This collection links analysis of violent and extremist narratives, not manifestos, propaganda archives, recruitment spaces, target lists, or tactical material.
- Do not investigate private people through doxxing, impersonation, stalking, intrusion, or mass contact. Preserve evidence lawfully and use qualified journalists, lawyers, researchers, or authorities when stakes are high.

### When to pause

Pause the investigation and involve a trusted person when it is disrupting sleep, work, food, relationships, or safety; producing escalating certainty without new evidence; or creating pressure to confront, expose, follow, or punish someone. Compassionate support can address fear and functioning while factual questions remain open. If there is immediate danger, use local emergency help; in the United States, the [988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline](../awesome-abundance/health-well-being-and-accessibility/README.md#crisis-and-immediate-support) is available by call, text, or chat.
