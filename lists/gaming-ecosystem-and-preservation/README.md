# Awesome Gaming Ecosystem and Preservation [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> Open play and creation, modding, accessibility, preservation, research, community well-being, and consumer safety across games.

Part of [akashic](../../README.md), the Ego Hygiene knowledge collection.

This collection gives players, creators, preservationists, researchers, families, and community stewards distinct canonical starting points without treating commercial games as freely reusable material. Open-source emulators, engines, tools, and metadata do not make the games they work with open. Game code, artwork, music, ROMs, disc images, firmware, manuals, and trademarks may remain copyrighted; buying a copy or finding a work unavailable for sale does not automatically authorize copying, circumvention, or redistribution, and "abandonware" is not a license. Laws and preservation exemptions vary by jurisdiction and purpose, so check current rules and seek qualified legal advice when necessary.

Mods likewise remain subject to the original game's license and terms as well as each contributor's permissions. A hosting platform does not grant rights to reuse third-party assets, bypass access controls, evade anti-cheat systems, or disrupt online play. Accessibility tags and reviews are discovery aids rather than guarantees; verify controls, hardware compatibility, settings, language, region, and the disabled player's own requirements before purchase.

Descriptions identify platform, cost, account, region, age, license, and reuse constraints where a source publishes them. Monetization and gambling-like mechanics are documented for consumer understanding, not promoted, and clinical references are not tools for diagnosing ordinary enthusiasm or play. Terms, storefronts, laws, and platform features change; this first edition was reviewed on **2026-09-02**.

Game engines and general production software, including Godot, belong in [Creative Tools and Production](../awesome-abundance/creative-tools-and-production/README.md#3d-games-cad-and-making) and [Free and Open-Source Software](../awesome-abundance/free-and-open-source-software/README.md#creative-work). Reusable audio, art, and models, including Freesound, Kenney Assets, and OpenGameArt, belong in [Creative Commons and Media](../awesome-abundance/creative-commons-and-media/README.md#3d-and-game-assets). AbleGamers services belong in [Health, Well-Being, and Accessibility](../awesome-abundance/health-well-being-and-accessibility/README.md#assistive-technology-and-disability-services); broader account defense belongs in [Security](../security/README.md#account-security-and-incident-readiness), and general mental-health care belongs in [Health and Well-Being](../health-and-well-being/README.md).

## Open Games and Player Tools

- [F-Droid Games](https://f-droid.org/en/categories/games/) - Android catalog of free and open-source games; installation is free, individual licenses and device requirements vary, and no F-Droid account is required.
- [Flathub Games](https://flathub.org/apps/category/Game) - Linux Flatpak catalog mixing open-source and proprietary games; prices, licenses, accounts, and controller support vary by application.
- [Heroic Games Launcher](https://heroicgameslauncher.com/) - GPLv3 launcher for Linux, macOS, and Windows that connects to supported commercial libraries; store accounts and ownership of the games remain required.
- [LibreGameWiki](https://libregamewiki.org/Main_Page) - Community wiki focused on games whose code and media meet free-culture criteria, with platform and license information where documented.
- [Lutris](https://lutris.net/) - GPL-licensed Linux game manager for native titles, emulators, and connected stores; browsing is free, while contributions and some integrations require accounts and separately licensed games.
- [Open Source Game Clones](https://osgameclones.com/) - Community-maintained comparison of open-source remakes and reimplementations, including engine status, code license, and whether original proprietary data is required.
- [Open Source Games List](https://trilarion.github.io/opensourcegames/) - Searchable catalog of open-source games with platform, genre, engine, and repository links; each project's code and content licenses should be checked separately.
- [Playnite](https://playnite.link/) - MIT-licensed Windows library manager that unifies installed games, emulators, and store integrations; third-party accounts, metadata, and game licenses remain separate.

## Creation, Learning, and Distribution

- [CS50's Introduction to Game Development](https://cs50.harvard.edu/games/2018/) - Free archived Harvard course using Lua, LÖVE, and Unity to teach 2D and 3D game patterns; lectures and assignments are publicly accessible without an account.
- [Game Programming Patterns](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/) - Free-to-read web book explaining reusable architecture patterns for game code; the text is copyrighted and example-code terms should be checked before reuse.
- [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/) - Conference archive of game-development talks spanning design, production, art, audio, narrative, business, and engineering; some sessions are free and others require paid access or an account.
- [Global Game Jam](https://globalgamejam.org/) - Worldwide network of locally organized game-making events with online resources; an account is required, users must be at least 13, and fees, language, venue access, and minor-participation rules vary by site.
- [International Game Developers Association](https://igda.org/) - Global professional association with chapters, special-interest groups, events, ethics guidance, and career resources; some participation and benefits require membership or local registration.
- [MDN Game Development](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games) - Free web-game tutorials and references covering Canvas, WebGL, Web Audio, APIs, performance, and publishing under Mozilla's documented content and code licenses.
- [The Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/) - Free web textbook on spatial design, blockouts, playtesting, and production practice, published under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

Use the existing Akashic collections linked above for engines, production software, licensed assets, and distribution through itch.io instead of duplicating those entries here.

## Modding and Interoperability

- [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/) - Cross-platform discovery and hosting community for mods, engines, tutorials, and development updates; browsing is free, posting requires an account, and file permissions vary by creator and game.
- [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/) - Mod, plugin, data-pack, and shader platform centered on Minecraft and supported games; downloads are generally free, publishing requires an account, and every project's license and dependencies differ.
- [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/) - Large mod-hosting platform with free and paid account tiers; author permissions, game requirements, mature-content access, and reuse rights vary by file.
- [OpenMW](https://openmw.org/) - GPL-licensed cross-platform reimplementation of the Morrowind engine and editor that requires legally obtained original game data for normal use.
- [OpenRCT2](https://openrct2.io/) - GPL-licensed reimplementation of RollerCoaster Tycoon 2 for major desktop platforms; most installations require files from a legally obtained copy of the original game.
- [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Home) - Community wiki documenting PC-game fixes, configuration, compatibility, and availability; reading is free, editing requires an account, and third-party fixes retain their own terms.
- [ProtonDB](https://www.protondb.com/) - Crowdsourced Linux and Steam Deck compatibility reports for Steam games; browsing is free and submitting reports requires Steam authentication.

## Accessibility and Adaptive Play

- [Accessible Games Initiative](https://accessiblegames.com/) - Voluntary industry tag vocabulary for communicating game accessibility features before purchase; a tag describes a feature and is not independent conformance testing.
- [Blind Gaming Resources](https://blindgaming.no/en/resources/) - Free English-language, screen-reader-friendly guidance for blind and low-vision players and developers, including implementation, testing, platform, and community references.
- [Can I Play That?](https://caniplaythat.com/) - Accessibility reviews, previews, and reference guides written from disabled-player perspectives; coverage and tested platforms vary by game.
- [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/) - Free practical reference organizing inclusive game-design guidance into basic, intermediate, and advanced considerations with examples.
- [GameAccess](https://gameaccess.info/) - SpecialEffect's free video-game accessibility resources covering adaptive equipment, control methods, eye gaze, switches, and individual game setups.
- [Gaming Accessibility Fundamentals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/gaming-accessibility-fundamentals/) - Free Microsoft Learn path for planning, designing, testing, and communicating accessible games; no purchase is required and sign-in is optional for progress tracking.
- [PlayStation Accessibility](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/accessibility/) - Official overview of accessibility features and tags for supported PlayStation hardware and software; availability varies by console, game, accessory, language, and region.
- [Xbox Accessibility](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/community/for-everyone/accessibility) - Official hub for Xbox accessibility settings, adaptive hardware, support, and developer guidance; device, game, account, and regional requirements vary.

## Preservation, Emulation, and Legal Boundaries

- [DOSBox Staging](https://www.dosbox-staging.org/) - GPL-2.0-or-later DOS emulator for Linux, macOS, and Windows; the emulator is free, but users must supply game files they are authorized to use.
- [Flashpoint Archive](https://flashpointarchive.org/) - Community project preserving web games and animations with free desktop access; software licenses, archived-content rights, takedown requests, and creator permissions remain distinct.
- [Internet Archive Software Collection](https://archive.org/details/software) - Publicly accessible collection of historical software and related media for research and preservation; access mode, item rights, region, and reuse permissions vary by record.
- [Library of Congress Recommended Formats for Software and Video Games](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/resources/rfs/software-videogames.html) - U.S. preservation guidance ranking preferred technical characteristics and documentation for software and video-game acquisitions; it does not grant copying rights.
- [MAME FAQ](https://docs.mamedev.org/usingmame/commonissues.html) - Official emulator documentation that explains ROM requirements and states that copyrighted ROMs generally require authorization and that "abandonware" has no legal force.
- [No-Intro](https://no-intro.org/) - Preservation-oriented cataloging project that publishes identification metadata and DAT files rather than commercial ROM downloads; metadata and underlying game rights are separate.
- [Redump.org](https://redump.org/) - Community database of optical-disc checksums and technical metadata intended to document verified dumps, not distribute copyrighted disc images.
- [ScummVM FAQ](https://www.scummvm.org/faq/) - Official guidance for the GPL-licensed adventure-game interpreter, including the need for original data files unless a rights holder has approved a freeware release.
- [Software Preservation Network](https://www.softwarepreservationnetwork.org/) - International membership organization sharing research, training, policy work, and institutional practice for lawful software preservation and access.
- [U.S. Copyright Office Section 1201 Proceeding](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/) - Official 2024 U.S. rulemaking record for temporary, narrowly defined circumvention exemptions, including video-game and software preservation; it is jurisdiction-specific and not a general copying license.

## History, Archives, Research, and Criticism

- [Critical Distance](https://www.critical-distance.com/) - Curated weekly and thematic roundups of game criticism, blogs, video essays, and research conversations, free to read with source rights retained.
- [DiGRA Digital Library](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl) - Free-access proceedings and white papers from Digital Games Research Association conferences; article reuse terms should be checked per publication.
- [Game Studies](https://gamestudies.org/) - Free-access, peer-reviewed international journal of computer-game research; articles remain subject to the journal's stated copyright terms.
- [Games That Weren't](https://www.gamesthatwerent.com/) - Research archive documenting cancelled and unreleased games through articles, scans, interviews, and recovered material; access and reuse rights vary by item.
- [International Center for the History of Electronic Games](https://www.museumofplay.org/collections/icheg/) - The Strong museum's collection of games, hardware, design records, advertising, and player-created artifacts; research access, digitization, and rights vary.
- [MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/) - Commercial game database with credits, releases, platforms, screenshots, and technical metadata; browsing is free, contribution requires an account, and reuse or API access follows current terms.
- [Museum of the Moving Image Video Games Collection](https://movingimage.org/collection/collection-spotlight_video-games/) - Museum overview of playable games, hardware, source material, and preservation work; onsite and digital access and object rights vary.
- [Video Game History Foundation Digital Archive](https://archive.gamehistory.org/) - Free research portal for digitized magazines, ephemera, development material, and library collections; account, download, and reuse conditions vary by collection.
- [Well Played Journal](https://playstorypress.org/well-played-journal/) - Free-access journal using close readings and criticism to examine how games work and what play experiences mean; author and publication rights remain in force.

## Community, Families, and Healthy Play

- [Digital Thriving Playbook](https://digitalthrivingplaybook.org/) - Free methods, examples, and research-backed concepts for belonging, trust, safety, prosocial design, crisis response, and well-being in online communities; contributing requires an account.
- [ESRB Family Gaming Guide](https://www.esrb.org/tools-for-parents/family-gaming-guide/) - North American guide to ratings, content descriptors, interactive elements, parental controls, spending, privacy, and family conversations; ratings describe content, not quality or difficulty.
- [Family Media Plan](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/How-to-Make-a-Family-Media-Use-Plan.aspx) - American Academy of Pediatrics guidance for collaboratively setting family routines around media, sleep, purchases, privacy, communication, and other activities.
- [Internet Matters Online Gaming Advice](https://www.internetmatters.org/advice/by-activity/online-gaming-advice-hub/) - UK-focused family guidance on age ratings, chat, bullying, spending, privacy, scams, and parental controls across common gaming platforms.
- [PEGI](https://pegi.info/) - European age-rating and content-descriptor system used across participating countries; coverage is regional and ratings address suitability rather than skill or product quality.
- [Safe In Our World](https://safeinourworld.org/) - International games-industry charity offering mental-health information, training, stories, and signposting; resources are educational and not a substitute for professional or emergency care.
- [Take This](https://www.takethis.org/) - Mental-health nonprofit serving players and the games industry through education, research, workplace guidance, and safer-community programs; it does not provide individual clinical care.
- [Thriving in Games Group](https://thrivingingames.org/) - Industry collaboration, formerly the Fair Play Alliance, sharing research and practices for safer, more inclusive, and prosocial player communities.
- [WHO Gaming Disorder](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder) - World Health Organization explanation of the ICD-11 diagnosis, which requires significant impairment and applies to only a small proportion of players; seek a qualified clinician for assessment.

## Privacy, Accounts, Monetization, and Scams

- [CFPB Consumer Advisory on Video-Game Spending](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-advisory-video-games-are-targeting-your-children-to-get-into-your-wallet/) - Archived 2024 U.S. consumer advisory on virtual currencies, surprise charges, account theft, gambling-like design, parental controls, and behavioral or biometric data collection.
- [Epic Games Account Security](https://www.epicgames.com/help/account-c-45487929/a24989601) - Official guidance on unique passwords, authenticator-based two-factor authentication, email and linked-account security, phishing, shared devices, account sales, and false offers.
- [European Parliament Study on Loot Boxes](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/IPOL_STU%282020%29652727) - Free 2020 consumer-protection study examining loot-box design, youth exposure, spending, research evidence, national approaches, and possible EU policy responses.
- [FTC Gaming Cases and Consumer Advice](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/features/gaming) - U.S. regulator hub for video-game cases and guidance involving privacy, online safety, in-app purchases, refunds, children, and competition.
- [FTC Video Games, Loot Boxes, and Your Money](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2019/09/video-games-loot-boxes-your-money) - U.S. consumer explainer on random paid items, uncertain odds, youth spending, and purchase controls; it informs rather than endorses buying loot boxes.
- [PlayStation Security Best Practices](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/account/security-best-practice/) - Official account guidance covering passkeys, unique passwords, QR-code and direct-message scams, shared devices, purchase protection, and recovery; features vary by account and region.
- [Steam Account Security Recommendations](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/6639-EB3C-EC79-FF60) - Official advice on Steam Guard, unique passwords, email security, phishing, malware, trade and item scams, and recovering a compromised account.
- [UK Gambling Commission on Loot Boxes](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/loot-boxes-within-video-games) - UK regulator explanation of when game items may fall within gambling law and where consumer risks remain outside that boundary; classifications depend on current facts and law.
