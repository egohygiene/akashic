# Awesome Recipes, Cooking, and Food Data [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> Open and openly accessible recipe data, kitchen tools, cooking education, safety, preservation, affordability, and food history.

Part of [akashic](../../README.md), the Ego Hygiene knowledge collection.

"Open" has several meanings in this collection. **Open source** describes software with a source-code license; **open data** and **open content** require an explicit reuse license or public-domain status; **free access** means material can be read or used without payment but does not grant reuse rights; and **API access** means a service is programmable under its own keys, quotas, and terms. Check the source's current license before copying recipe prose, photographs, or datasets.

Dietary tags are discovery aids, not guarantees that a recipe is safe for a particular allergy or medical condition. Confirm ingredients, labels, cross-contact controls, and current professional guidance. Use current food-safety advice for cooking and preservation; historical recipes document their period and are not safety instructions. Culturally specific resources should retain their creators, communities, language, and historical context rather than being treated as interchangeable variations.

Where a source publishes them, descriptions call out cost, account, equipment, skill, dietary, language, regional, and reuse constraints so those dimensions remain searchable and can support future catalog filters. This first edition was reviewed on **2026-09-02**.

This collection covers finding, preparing, storing, documenting, and reusing food knowledge. Food-benefit and assistance navigation belongs in [Financial Relief and Assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#food-access-to-meals), with urgent gateways in [Public Services and Support](../public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance) and community food in [Awesome Abundance](../awesome-abundance/community-sharing-and-material-resources/README.md#food-and-household-essentials). General clinical nutrition and health guidance belongs in [Health and Well-Being](../health-and-well-being/README.md).

## From Food Access to a Meal

Start with one meal you can actually obtain, store, and prepare. If you need food, use [Food Access to Meals](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#food-access-to-meals) for food today, SNAP/WIC, school meals, and older-adult support. You can seek food today while a benefit application is pending.

### Match the food to your situation

| What you have available             | A practical next step                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No stove or only a microwave        | Try EatFresh's Limited Kitchen or No-cook filters in [Affordable and Accessible Cooking](#affordable-and-accessible-cooking). Check the actual tools, safe water, refrigeration, and instructions; no-cook does not mean no equipment or storage. |
| No reliable refrigerator            | Ask for unopened, shelf-stable, ready-to-eat food in portions you can use. Read storage and after-opening instructions; a shelf-stable package may need refrigeration once opened.                                                                |
| Little energy, time, money, or help | Choose a simple meal from ready-to-eat components, a short recipe, or a community meal. Start with what you can use safely; weekly batch cooking is optional.                                                                                     |
| No safe water or preparation space  | Ask a pantry, meal provider, or 211 for a prepared meal or food suited to these limits. Tell them about transport and accessibility needs before a trip.                                                                                          |

### Obtain → store safely → prepare → stretch

| Step         | Small checklist                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Obtain       | Confirm pickup or delivery, hours, cost, and what food is available. Ask about ingredients and storage history when relevant. Choose food you can carry, open, and use with your equipment; an unsuitable donation is not a usable meal.                                                                                                    |
| Store safely | Follow labels and food-specific storage guidance. Keep the refrigerator at 40°F / 4°C or below. Refrigerate perishables within two hours total out of safe temperature control, or one hour above 90°F / 32°C; include travel time. If safe cold storage is unavailable, ask for a suitable alternative.                                    |
| Prepare      | Clean hands, surfaces, and tools; separate raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs from ready-to-eat food. Follow the recipe and use a food thermometer with the food-specific temperature chart. Cooking does not remove allergen cross-contact.                                                                                              |
| Stretch      | Plan the next use before making extra. Divide perishable extras into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly, label dates, and use food-specific storage limits. Freeze suitable portions while still safe only if you have reliable freezer space. Count package costs, transport, utilities, and likely waste when comparing options. |

Safety sources: [Four Steps to Food Safety](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety), [safe minimum temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures), and [cold-food storage charts](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts). Do not use taste, smell, or appearance to decide whether food is safe after [a power outage](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage). FoodKeeper in [Food Safety](#food-safety) offers item-specific storage guidance; its browser tool needs JavaScript.

> **Allergy check:** filters cannot establish safety. Read each product label and ask the provider about ingredients, shared equipment, and handling; [FDA labeling guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) and [FARE's cross-contact guidance](https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/avoiding-cross-contact) explain the limits. If ingredients or cross-contact controls cannot be confirmed for your allergy, ask for a suitable alternative rather than guessing. Ask a qualified health professional about an individual medical diet.

## Open Data, Standards, and Reuse

- [AGROVOC](https://www.fao.org/agrovoc/) - FAO-managed multilingual linked-data thesaurus for agriculture, food, and related domains, published under CC BY 4.0.
- [Cooklang](https://cooklang.org/) - MIT-licensed, human-readable recipe markup language with offline files, ingredient scaling, timers, shopping lists, and command-line tooling.
- [FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) - USDA food-composition search and downloadable data published in the U.S. public domain under CC0, with source attribution requested.
- [FoodData Central API Guide](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide) - Documentation for REST access to USDA FoodData Central; requests require a data.gov API key and are subject to rate limits.
- [FoodOn](https://foodon.org/) - CC BY 4.0 farm-to-fork ontology for describing foods, ingredients, processes, packaging, and food-safety concepts across data systems.
- [Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/) - Crowdsourced global packaged-food database with ingredient, allergen, nutrition, label, and packaging records; the database uses ODbL, individual contents use the Database Contents License, and product images use CC BY-SA 3.0.
- [Open Food Facts API](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/) - Read-and-contribute API for Open Food Facts product data; clients must follow current reuse, authentication, and rate-limit requirements.
- [Schema.org Recipe](https://schema.org/Recipe) - Shared structured-data vocabulary for representing ingredients, instructions, times, yields, nutrition, and dietary suitability on the web.
- [Wikibooks Cookbook](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook) - Community-authored multilingual cookbook covering recipes, ingredients, equipment, and techniques under CC BY-SA 4.0 unless otherwise noted.

## Open Recipe and Kitchen Tools

- [Grocy](https://grocy.info/) - MIT-licensed self-hosted household system for groceries, pantry stock, recipes, meal plans, shopping lists, and expiration tracking.
- [KitchenOwl](https://kitchenowl.org/) - AGPL-licensed self-hosted grocery, recipe, meal-planning, household-expense, and shared-list application with mobile, web, and desktop clients.
- [Mealie](https://mealie.io/) - AGPL-licensed self-hosted recipe manager with website import, meal planning, shopping lists, household sharing, and a REST API.
- [Nextcloud Cookbook](https://apps.nextcloud.com/apps/cookbook) - AGPL-licensed Nextcloud application that stores recipes as Schema.org-compatible JSON files and can import supported recipe pages.
- [Recipya](https://recipya.musicavis.ca/) - AGPL-licensed cross-platform recipe manager with web and file import, paper-recipe digitization, cookbooks, printing, and measurement conversion.
- [RecipeSage](https://recipesage.com/) - AGPL-licensed hosted or self-hosted recipe keeper with document and website import, meal plans, shopping lists, collaboration, and offline-capable clients.
- [Tandoor Recipes](https://tandoor.dev/) - Source-available recipe, meal-plan, shopping-list, and sharing system licensed under AGPLv3 with a Commons Clause restriction, available through self-hosting or a quota-limited managed account.

## Preservation and Food-Waste Reduction

- [EPA Wasted Food Scale](https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-management-food/wasted-food-scale) - U.S. evidence-based hierarchy prioritizing prevention, donation, and upcycling before lower-benefit waste-management pathways.
- [FAO Food Loss and Waste Platform](https://www.fao.org/platform-food-loss-waste/en/) - Global measurement, policy, training, tools, and datasets for reducing food loss and waste across supply chains and households.
- [Freezing and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety) - USDA reference for safe freezing, thawing, refreezing, packaging, freezer storage, and power interruptions.
- [Let's Preserve](https://extension.psu.edu/food-safety-and-quality/home-food-preservation-and-safety/lets-preserve/) - Penn State Extension fact sheets in English and Spanish covering tested methods for preserving fruits, vegetables, and meats; some downloads may have a small fee.
- [National Center for Home Food Preservation](https://nchfp.uga.edu/) - Research-based USDA-supported recommendations for canning, freezing, drying, fermenting, pickling, curing, smoking, and storage.
- [USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning](https://nchfp.uga.edu/resources/category/usda-guide) - Free official guide to tested canning principles and processes, organized by food type and preservation method.

## Culture, History, and Attribution

- [Feeding America Historic Cookbook Dataset](https://lib.msu.edu/feedingamericadata) - Downloadable transcribed and TEI-like encoded data from 76 public-domain American cookbooks held by Michigan State University Libraries, with citation guidance.
- [Food and Foodways Web Archive](https://www.loc.gov/collections/food-and-foodways-web-archive/about-this-collection/) - Library of Congress archive of selected websites documenting food systems, recipes, sustainability, diaspora, and regional foodways; access and reuse vary by item.
- [Smithsonian Archives Center Cookbook Collection](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/sova-nmah-ac-0510) - Finding aid for cookbooks, recipe booklets, cards, clippings, and records concerning food customs; digitization and rights vary by object.
- [Smithsonian Food History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/topics/food) - Exhibitions, collections, and research using food and agriculture to examine technological, cultural, labor, migration, and social history in the United States.
- [The Recipes Project](https://recipes.hypotheses.org/about) - International scholarly community publishing contextualized research on recipes across food, medicine, art, science, magic, and material culture.
- [The Sifter](https://thesifter.org/) - Free public database for locating and comparing historical and contemporary food writing across languages; records describe sources rather than granting reuse rights.
- [What's on the Menu?](https://www.nypl.org/research/support/whats-on-the-menu) - New York Public Library's digitized historical restaurant-menu collection with images, transcriptions, and downloadable data; item rights and completeness vary.
