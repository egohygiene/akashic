# Awesome Business and Entrepreneurship [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> A plain-language map of how a business works, what rules attach to it, which official paths are free, and when a paid service is only a convenience layer.

Part of [akashic](../../README.md), the Ego Hygiene knowledge collection.

<!-- site-guide:start -->

> **Scope and safety:** Most resources below are United States-oriented, with country-specific gateways near the end. Business activity can create tax, licensing, consumer-protection, zoning, privacy, and employment duties before someone forms an LLC. An LLC or corporation can separate some risks, but it does not replace insurance, contracts, records, required filings, or professional advice, and it does not erase personal guarantees or liability for a person's own conduct. Rules change and vary by state, locality, industry, product, and facts; verify consequential decisions with the controlling agency and a qualified legal, tax, insurance, or financial professional. This guide is education, not individualized advice.

**Access language:** **Official** means a government or regulator source. **No-cost** means the resource itself has no fee, although filing, tax, postage, payment-processing, or professional costs may still apply. **Free tier** means an ongoing limited plan. **Pay-per-use** means no recurring base subscription but charges attach to transactions or orders. **Paid** services should be treated as optional until the underlying obligation is understood.

## Orient First

### The business system in one view

| Layer | Question to answer | What exists underneath the apps and services |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer | What specific result, product, or experience is being sold, to whom, and for how much? | Customer conversations, a written scope or product specification, costs, price, and a way to deliver. |
| Permission | Is the activity allowed here and in this form? | Zoning, professional and local licenses, product rules, labeling, tax registration, platform rules, and landlord or association restrictions. |
| Structure | Who owns the activity, signs contracts, receives profit, and bears risk? | A sole proprietorship, partnership, state-law entity, cooperative, nonprofit, or corporation, plus any tax elections. |
| Identity | How do agencies, banks, vendors, and customers identify it? | Legal name, DBA, state registration, EIN or other tax ID, addresses, registered agent, accounts, and verified contact details. |
| Money | How is every dollar traced and every obligation funded? | Separate accounts where appropriate, invoices or checkout, bookkeeping, receipts, cash-flow planning, tax reserves, reconciliations, and reports. |
| Risk | What could hurt a customer, worker, owner, or the public? | Contracts, insurance, safety controls, accessibility, privacy and security practices, quality checks, and incident response. |
| Operations | How does a promise become repeatable delivery? | A calendar, task or order system, inventory and vendor records, documented processes, backups, support, and renewal deadlines. |
| Change | How can the business pause, grow, add people, move, sell, or end cleanly? | Amendments, payroll, foreign qualification, succession, contract transfer, final returns, account closure, dissolution, and retained records. |

### Choose the smallest relevant path

**Testing an idea.** Interview potential customers, price one concrete offer, calculate contribution margin, and check the permission and risk gates before buying a formation package or a software stack.

**Solo service or freelance work.** Start with a written scope, invoice or checkout path, records, tax plan, separate money trail, insurance check, and the structure appropriate to the actual risk.

**Online, creator, or digital-product business.** Add platform and payment fees, refunds, intellectual-property rights, accessibility, email and advertising rules, privacy, chargebacks, and reliable exports.

**Physical product, food, cosmetics, alcohol, or children's product.** Do not sell first and research later; product, labeling, testing, facility, and local health rules can control the entire plan.

**Home-based or local business.** Add zoning, occupancy or home-occupation rules, signs, local permits, sales tax, waste, fire and health requirements, and eligibility rules for map listings.

**Hiring or using contractors.** Classification is not a preference or a contract label. Add payroll and tax accounts, I-9, new-hire reporting, labor rules, posters, safety, insurance, and state tests before the first person starts.

**Investor-backed startup.** Venture financing, equity, securities, intellectual-property assignment, and Delaware structures are specialist paths, not default advice for a local or self-funded business.

### What a consolidation service is actually doing

| Packaged feature | Underlying task | Before paying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entity formation | A filing with a state or Tribal authority. | Compare the direct form and mandatory government fee with the service fee, add-ons, turnaround claim, and renewal terms. |
| EIN service | A no-cost application to the IRS for eligible U.S. applicants. | Form the state entity first when applicable; the IRS explicitly says never pay for the EIN itself. |
| Registered agent | A state-law role for receiving legal and official documents at an eligible address during required hours. | Check whether self-service is allowed, what address becomes public, how documents are delivered, and the recurring price. |
| DBA or name filing | A state, county, or city registration that may let the business use another name. | A DBA does not create a liability entity, and a domain or state name filing does not guarantee trademark rights. |
| Annual-report compliance | A recurring state filing, fee, franchise tax, or renewal calendar. | Read what the plan monitors, what it files, which government fees are extra, and how to export documents or cancel. |
| Licenses and permits | Permissions issued by profession-, product-, activity-, and location-specific agencies. | A search report is not a license or blanket approval; verify each result with the issuing agency. |
| Tax treatment | Federal, state, and local classifications, elections, filings, and payment duties. | An LLC is not itself a federal tax classification, and an S corporation is generally a tax election with eligibility and payroll consequences. |
| Banking, books, payroll, or payments | Regulated accounts, records, calculations, filings, and movement of money. | Compare fees, data portability, holds, partner banks, support, security, and the cost of leaving. |

Record the official account owner, renewal date, cancellation method, price after any introductory period, government login, recovery method, and export location for every service. Keep formation documents, signed agreements, tax filings, bank statements, and licenses somewhere the vendor cannot lock.

## Money, Records, and Taxes

## Sell and Get Paid

## Funding Without Fantasy

Money sources are not interchangeable: revenue is earned, grants are restricted awards, loans must be repaid, and equity or investment crowdfunding sells ownership or securities rights. Model cash flow, total repayment, dilution, personal guarantees, collateral, default, taxes, reporting, and the cost of delay. Ordinary startup and expansion grants are uncommon; never pay someone who guarantees a government grant.

- [CDFI Fund Certified CDFI List](https://www.cdfifund.gov/certification) - Official certification information and lists for community development financial institutions that may serve borrowers overlooked by conventional lenders.
- [Kiva U.S. Borrower Loans](https://www.kiva.org/borrow/us-businesses) - Nonprofit zero-interest loan path for eligible United States businesses; approval, exclusions, public fundraising, account, network, and repayment requirements apply, and it is not free money.
- [SBA Grants](https://www.sba.gov/loans/additional-funding-opportunities/grants/) - Explains that SBA does not provide grants for ordinary business startup or expansion and identifies narrow research, exporting, and partner-program pathways.
- [SBA Lender Match](https://www.sba.gov/loans/lender-match/) - No-cost referral tool for participating SBA-approved lenders; it is not an application, approval, rate guarantee, or substitute for comparing terms.
- [SBA Loans and Additional Funding](https://www.sba.gov/loans/) - Official comparison of loans, investment capital, surety bonds, and limited grants; loans are debt and eligibility varies.
- [SBA Microloan Program](https://www.sba.gov/loans/microloans/) - Official smaller-loan program delivered through nonprofit intermediaries with local underwriting, training, uses, rates, and collateral rules.
- [SEC Regulation Crowdfunding Guide for Issuers](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/regulation-crowdfunding-guidance-issuers) - Official issuer guide to regulated investment crowdfunding, intermediary, disclosure, reporting, advertising, and investor-limit rules.
- [Treasury SSBCI Small Business Programs](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/small-business-programs/state-small-business-credit-initiative-ssbci/ssbci-2-program-information/small-business) - Routes eligible businesses to state, territory, and Tribal credit, investment, and technical-assistance programs; Treasury does not award a direct startup grant here.

## Change, Sell, or Close

Closing a federal tax account does not dissolve a state entity. Check every formation and foreign-qualification state, tax and payroll account, sales-tax permit, local license, contract, employee, creditor, asset, data-retention duty, and record before assuming the business is gone.

- [IRS Closing a Business](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/closing-a-business) - Official final-return, employee, tax, contractor, account, and recordkeeping checklist; use current tax-year instructions where its generic thresholds conflict.
- [IRS If You No Longer Need Your EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/if-you-no-longer-need-your-ein) - Explains that an EIN is permanent and the IRS deactivates the associated account rather than canceling or reusing the number.
- [SBA Close or Sell Your Business](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/manage-your-business/#close-or-sell-your-business) - Official orientation to sale valuation, ownership transfer, dissolution, registrations, taxes, employees, and retained records.
- [U.S. Courts Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics) - Neutral federal orientation to bankruptcy chapters, process, discharge, and court roles; insolvency decisions require timely qualified legal advice.
- [U.S. Courts Chapter 11 Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-11-bankruptcy-basics) - Official overview of Chapter 11, small-business cases, and Subchapter V; eligibility and strategic consequences can change.
