## Permission and Safe Practice

### Authorization vocabulary

Every entry records an `authorization` value. It describes the minimum context for responsible use, not a grant of permission.

| Value | Appropriate context |
| --- | --- |
| `reference-only` | Read standards, policy, advisories, datasets, or educational material without interacting with a target. |
| `isolated-lab` | Use only inside a deliberately vulnerable provider sandbox or a segregated local environment with disposable data. |
| `owned-systems` | Assess a system you own and control after confirming that providers, users, and connected services permit the activity. |
| `explicit-scope` | Work under written authorization that names targets, methods, timing, data rules, reporting channels, and stop conditions. |

### Operational-risk vocabulary

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | Reading or planning has no direct target interaction. |
| `low` | The resource supports bounded observation or design work, but sensitive information still needs care. |
| `moderate` | The resource can change systems, generate test traffic, or expose security-relevant findings. |
| `high` | The resource can actively probe targets or teach techniques that could cause material harm outside a lab or explicit scope. |

Risk describes potential misuse or operational impact. It does not imply that a high-risk resource is inappropriate inside its recorded authorization context.

### Before using any lab or tool

1. Write down the defensive objective, authorized targets, allowed methods, time window, data rules, rate limits, stop conditions, and the person who approved the work.
2. Prefer a provider-hosted sandbox or a segregated virtual machine or container network. Do not expose intentionally vulnerable services to the public internet or a production network.
3. Use disposable accounts and synthetic data. Keep real credentials, access tokens, personal data, client material, and production backups out of the lab.
4. For cloud labs, use a separate account or project with least-privilege roles, spending alerts, no production trust relationships, and a tested teardown plan.
5. Snapshot before changes, preserve only the evidence needed for the approved purpose, treat logs and findings as sensitive, and remove lab resources when finished.
6. Stop on unexpected access, data, instability, third-party infrastructure, or scope ambiguity. Do not escalate merely to prove greater impact.

### Reporting and disclosure

Collect the minimum sanitized evidence needed to reproduce and remediate the issue. Use the organization's published vulnerability-disclosure policy or `security.txt`, follow its encryption and communication instructions, and include affected versions, bounded reproduction facts, impact, and suggested mitigation. Do not publish secrets, personal data, exploit payloads, or details that create unnecessary risk. Coordinate timing with the affected party, and seek qualified legal advice when authorization or disclosure obligations are unclear.

### How the metadata supports decisions

Each entry records its source type (`authority`), access or cost model, platform, license, maintenance status, volatility, review cadence, skill level, authorized context, and operational risk. `linkStatus` and `linkChecked` only record mechanical availability; they are not a human truth review. Entries intentionally omit `reviewed` until a person performs and records that review.

## Suggestions and Contributions

Additions must default to defensive education, standards, hardening, authorized assessment, safe labs, monitoring, incident response, coordinated disclosure, public advisories, or career development. Do not submit resources centered on unauthorized access, credential theft, persistence, evasion, malware deployment, destructive activity, stolen data, live-target practice, exploit payloads, or rankings based on misuse capability.

Every new entry needs a canonical URL, a globally unique stable `akashic-meta` ID, accurate source, access, platform, account, license, maintenance, volatility, review-cadence, sensitivity, `authorization`, `operationalRisk`, and `skillLevel` fields, plus a description that states material scope, data-handling, platform, or cost limits. High-risk or dual-use resources require an isolated-lab or explicit-scope rationale and a capability-level description without copy-paste misuse guidance. Do not set `reviewed` unless a person has performed and recorded the required truth review. Read the [contribution guidelines](../../contributing.md) before opening an issue or pull request.
