## Access Key

| Access type | Meaning                                                                                                   |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open        | Free to use and released under an open license or into the public domain.                                 |
| Public      | Funded or operated as a public resource with no usage fee.                                                |
| Account     | Free access requires creating an account.                                                                 |
| Library     | Access depends on a participating library or library card.                                                |
| Eligibility | Access depends on residency, income, age, disability, education, service, or another published criterion. |
| Local       | Availability varies by location, inventory, schedule, or participating organization.                      |
| Free tier   | A continuing no-cost plan exists with documented limits; temporary trials do not qualify.                 |

## Disability Access and Independent Living

> **U.S. first edition:** Start with the activity you want to do, the barrier, and the support you choose. Independent living can include directing your own assistance; it does not mean doing everything alone. You decide whom to involve and what to share. A disability-service referral, an accommodation request, and an SSI/SSDI application use different processes; receiving cash benefits is not a universal prerequisite for access help. For immediate danger or urgent basic needs, use [Public Services](../public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance).

### Check the service and access arrangement

Centers for Independent Living are disability-led, nonresidential organizations, not residential facilities. Ask about peer support, skills, advocacy, transition help, and local personal-assistance or community-service referrals. Confirm intake, service area, capacity, costs, waiting lists, and who provides ongoing help. If you want aging or caregiver-specific support, use [Family's guide](../family-caregiving-and-aging/README.md#plan-aging-and-caregiver-support); needing access does not imply needing a caregiver.

**Free information is not a promise of free equipment, rides, or ongoing services.** A device loan means borrowing equipment; a financial loan means debt. Ask AT programs about demonstration, reuse, and funding options separately. Public reading and directory browsing do not require proving benefit eligibility; the actual service may have an assessment or application. Paratransit certification and trip booking are separate; ask the local operator about current requirements and a backup plan for missed transport.

**Choose communication that works for you.** Ask for readable electronic documents, large print, plain-language explanations, captioning, a qualified interpreter, relay, or time to use your communication device as needed. Confirm language and format rather than assuming a website or provider supports them. Telephone relay and an interpreter for a meeting are different services. For duties and exceptions in a particular setting, use the Legal reference above and qualified assistance.

**If an online route is difficult:** the ILRU directory lists centers by state/territory with provider contacts; AT3 lists program phone/email contacts. The ADA National Network advertises voice/relay at **800-949-4232** and an online question form; JAN publishes phone and email contact routes. DIAL's current site is a location-search locator; this guide does not promise the former hotline's availability. Ask each provider about language support, hours, and a non-web alternative. Source observations: September 18, 2026.

### Request, document, follow up, escalate

**1. Request:** describe the activity, barrier, preferred change, and relevant appointment or deadline. Ask the responsible office how to request access in your preferred communication format. A written record can help; do not assume every setting requires a particular form or a written request. You may choose a helper while retaining control over decisions and information sharing.

**2. Document:** privately save the request, date, recipient, response, confirmation, and notice deadlines. Before sending medical or identity records, ask why they are needed, who can see them, how they are retained, and how to send only what the process requires securely. Avoid sending a full history by default. JAN publishes a confidentiality statement and options for limiting identifying information; other providers have their own policies. Do not put personal records in public issues or shared examples.

**3. Follow up:** agree on a response date, contact route, and next step. Confirm that the proposed arrangement works for your activity; ask about an interim option or alternative if it does not. If refused, request the reason and available review process in an accessible format. Keep separate application, appeal, court, and service deadlines visible; an unanswered request does not automatically extend them.

**4. Escalate or get human help:** a CIL can help you explore self-advocacy; ADA specialists and JAN provide technical assistance. For advocacy or a disputed right, use [NDRN's Protection and Advocacy / Client Assistance locator, the ADA complaint router, or qualified legal help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#disability-and-language-access). Ask about issue scope, eligibility, confidentiality, capacity, and representation. Housing, employment, school, transit, and government services have different authorities; a general complaint is not a substitute for the correct appeal. Seek help promptly when a deadline or immediate loss of access is involved; you do not need to finish this checklist first.

**Small private record — synthetic example:** activity: community class; barrier: scanned handout; request: readable electronic copy; sent to: program access contact; follow-up: agreed date; outcome: verify the replacement works. Keep names, diagnoses, account numbers, and actual documents out of public examples. Use [Atlas](../../atlas/README.md) for existing place-specific routes, then verify the provider's current area and process.

## Curation Rules

- Include only legitimate and reasonably trustworthy access paths.
- Explain the important access condition in each description.
- Prefer official providers, public institutions, nonprofits, and established open communities.
- Link to the page where a reader can actually search, apply, download, borrow, or participate.
- Distinguish public-domain and open-license material from content that is merely free to view.
- Exclude piracy, referral funnels, sweepstakes, deceptive lead generation, and surprise-payment offers.
- Cover unauthorized shadow libraries only through neutral encyclopedia references that explain their legal status, never through direct access links.
- Exclude temporary free trials unless the resource is itself a recurring public event or published benefit.
- Treat availability, eligibility, quotas, and geographic restrictions as facts that require maintenance.
- Verify health, legal, financial, and public-benefit resources against authoritative sources.
