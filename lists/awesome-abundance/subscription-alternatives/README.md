# Awesome Subscription Alternatives [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> A practical guide to auditing recurring charges, stopping unwanted renewals, and replacing paid subscriptions with trustworthy no-cost access.

Part of [Awesome Abundance](../README.md) and [akashic](../../../README.md), the Ego Hygiene knowledge collection.

Free does not automatically mean safe, private, or sustainable. Prefer public resources, libraries, nonprofit services, open-source software, and clearly documented continuing free tiers. Avoid temporary trials, referral funnels, pirated access, and services that hide their limits or business model.

## Start Here

1. Review bank, credit-card, app-store, PayPal, and email records for recurring charges.
2. Record the provider, amount, renewal date, billing channel, and cancellation path.
3. Turn off auto-renewal before testing a replacement; confirm whether access continues through the paid term.
4. Save the cancellation confirmation and watch the next statement for another charge.
5. Replace the underlying need, not necessarily the same product. A library, built-in device feature, open-source application, public program, or existing bundle may already provide it.

Stopping a payment through a bank or payment processor does not necessarily cancel the underlying contract. Contact the provider first when possible, preserve records, and use formal payment controls or disputes when a company continues charging without authorization.

## Cancel and Control Renewals

- [Amazon Memberships and Subscriptions](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GU8AWX4GDKM7QGTE) - Official instructions for locating and canceling subscriptions billed through Amazon; services billed by another provider must be canceled with that provider.
- [Apple Subscription Cancellation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428) - Official steps for reviewing and canceling subscriptions billed through an Apple Account on supported devices; an expiration message indicates renewal is already off.
- [CFPB Stop Automatic Payments](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-stop-automatic-payments-from-my-bank-account-en-2023/) - United States guidance for revoking a company's debit authorization, notifying a bank or credit union, using stop-payment orders, and preserving records; fees and timing rules may apply.
- [FTC Free Trials and Auto-Renewals](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions) - United States consumer guidance for evaluating recurring offers, canceling subscriptions, documenting the request, monitoring statements, and disputing unauthorized charges.
- [Google Play Subscription Management](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/7018481) - Official instructions for canceling, pausing, restarting, or changing eligible Google Play subscriptions; uninstalling an app does not cancel its subscription.
- [Microsoft Subscription Cancellation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/subscriptions/cancel-your-microsoft-subscription) - Official cancellation flow for subscriptions purchased directly from Microsoft; purchases through app stores or other sellers must be managed through the billing provider.
- [PayPal Automatic Payments](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-is-an-automatic-payment-and-how-do-i-update-or-cancel-one-help240) - Official controls for reviewing merchants with recurring PayPal authorization, canceling an automatic payment, and changing its payment method.
- [Roku Subscription Management](https://support.roku.com/article/manage-or-cancel-subscriptions) - Official instructions for finding and turning off auto-renewal for subscriptions billed through Roku; direct-billed services must be managed with their provider.

## Evaluate Free Services

Before relying on a free replacement, verify each of the following dimensions.

**Continuity:** Confirm that it is a continuing no-cost plan, public benefit, or open-source project rather than a temporary trial.

**Business model:** Determine whether it is funded by donations, taxes, memberships, paid upgrades, advertising, data collection, or another disclosed source.

**Limits:** Check data caps, storage, devices, locations, export formats, inactivity deletion, advertising, and feature restrictions.

**Privacy:** Determine what activity, identifiers, files, or metadata the operator can collect and whether sensitive use is appropriate.

**Billing safety:** Prefer services that require no payment method. Where billing is possible, use hard limits and alerts rather than relying on memory.

**Portability:** Confirm that data can be exported in a usable format before committing significant work.

**Fit:** A free option can be excellent for ordinary use while remaining unsuitable for streaming, high-volume storage, regulated data, professional collaboration, or a high-risk threat model.
