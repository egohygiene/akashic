## Evidence Workflow

1. Confirm the legal and organizational authority, case identifier, decision question, sources, methods, time window, data minimization rules, retention, disclosure path, approver, and stop conditions before touching a device or account.
2. Prepare trusted workstations and sterile media; record tool versions, configuration, clock sources, operator, and environment. Validate a tool against known data when the result or method materially affects the conclusion.
3. Acquire the least invasive source that can answer the authorized question. Record unavoidable changes during live collection and stop on unexpected third-party data, scope ambiguity, instability, or access-control barriers.
4. Hash the acquisition and verified copies, preserve the original read-only, and analyze a working copy. Record source and destination identifiers, handlers, timestamps, transfers, storage protections, and every integrity check.
5. Normalize time zones explicitly; retain parser logs and errors; distinguish observed facts, tool interpretations, and investigator inferences. Corroborate material findings with another artifact, method, or independently implemented tool when practical.
6. Have another qualified person review the methods, evidence trail, assumptions, limitations, and report. State missing sources, parsing failures, uncertainty, and alternative explanations instead of overstating the result.
7. Share only the minimum authorized material, through approved encrypted channels, then retain or dispose of evidence and working data under the governing policy and law. Never upload real case evidence, secrets, or personal data to public training systems or repository issues.

A matching hash supports byte-for-byte integrity between the values compared; it does not by itself prove provenance, truth, completeness, or lawful handling. Tool output is not automatically fact, and this collection does not determine courtroom admissibility or professional qualification in any jurisdiction.

## Metadata Guide

Every entry records its evidence family in `artifactDomain` and its workflow functions in `forensicRole`. `authorization` describes the minimum permission context, while `operationalRisk` describes potential harm outside it. `status`, `volatility`, and `reviewTier` expose maintenance state and review cadence; `linkStatus` and `linkChecked` record only mechanical availability, not a human truth review.

| Field | How to read it |
| --- | --- |
| `artifactDomain` | Evidence families such as disk, filesystem, memory, network, browser, mobile, cloud, container, media, logs, malware, or multi-domain. |
| `forensicRole` | Acquisition, preservation, examination, analysis, timeline, indexing/search, correlation/visualization, validation, case management, reporting, or education. |
| `authorization` | `reference-only` for reading; `isolated-lab` for synthetic training; `owned-systems` for sources you control; `explicit-scope` for written authority over named sources and methods. |
| `status` | `active` resources are presently maintained or operational; `historical` references remain useful but are not current practice; `archived` software is retained only with an explicit limitation. |

Commercial claims such as “forensically sound,” evidentiary reliability, or legal defensibility are not adopted by inclusion. Validate tools and methods for the specific source, version, environment, question, and governing quality system.
