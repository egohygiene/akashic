# Emergency essentials checklist — print and keep privately

**United States first edition · September 23, 2026.** Print this page before an emergency and mark a paper copy by hand. It is a starting list, not a supply guarantee or a live safety instruction. Adjust quantities and contents to your household, the hazard, your means, and [Ready.gov's kit guidance](https://www.ready.gov/kit). In an active event, follow current local officials; call 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency when possible. [Read the before / during / after guide](emergency-preparedness-and-recovery.md).

## People, contacts, and alerts

- [ ] Agree on one out-of-area contact, a nearby meeting point, and a backup place; explain the plan in a way each person can use.
- [ ] Check school, work, caregiver, and building reunification procedures; decide who helps each dependent person or animal.
- [ ] Find the official local emergency office, its alert signup and phone route, and an accessible or translated alternative.
- [ ] Check Wireless Emergency Alert settings and a second channel such as radio; confirm NOAA Weather Radio reception before relying on it.
- [ ] Decide how to get to a safe, accessible destination if evacuation is ordered, including a transport backup without a car.

## Supplies to gather or arrange

- [ ] Water and food that fit each household member and pet; check storage, dietary needs, and expiration.
- [ ] Medicines, a current medication list, glasses/hearing items, hygiene and first-aid supplies; ask a clinician or pharmacist about special storage and refills.
- [ ] Mobility, communication, and medical equipment; ask the supplier and care team about safe power and transport backups.
- [ ] Phone, charging cables, charged power source, light, and a usable radio/receiver if available; test them periodically.
- [ ] Clothing, shoes, weather protection, child or older-adult supplies, and pet carrier/ID/supplies as applicable.
- [ ] Protected copies of key identity, insurance, housing, and care documents; some payment method if available. Keep private information secure.

## Two places and a recovery folder

- [ ] Keep an easy-to-reach set for leaving and a separate shelter-at-home plan; know who can carry the supplies.
- [ ] Ask the local office about a current shelter and its access, animal, language, and transport options. A prior listing is not a reservation.
- [ ] Identify a private place for insurance details, property inventory, receipts, emergency costs, notices, and later application confirmations.
- [ ] Review this plan when household needs, medications, equipment, contacts, seasons, or location change.

**Fill only a private paper copy:** local alert office and number; out-of-area contact; meeting places; transport and shelter questions; care and equipment contacts. Do not post the completed sheet, names, phone numbers, medical details, or claim numbers online. If supplies are unaffordable, start with what you already have and ask a local emergency office or [211](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance) about current help. During an event, official instructions take priority over every item above.
