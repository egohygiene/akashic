# Emergency preparedness and disaster recovery

**United States first edition · sources checked September 23, 2026.** This guide offers a small before / during / after path. It is not a live alert, shelter roster, forecast, evacuation order, or eligibility decision. [Public Services and Support](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery) holds the short route and official starting points.

> **If an event is happening:** Follow current instructions from your local emergency management and public safety officials. For immediate danger or a medical emergency in the United States, call 911 when possible. A static guide cannot tell you whether to evacuate, shelter, enter a building, travel on a route, or return home during a particular incident.

**One next action before an event:** Find your local official alert channel, then agree with your household on one out-of-area contact and a way to reconnect. [Print the essentials checklist](emergency-essentials-checklist.md) and fill your private copy by hand; it works without an account or network connection.

## Before: plan around your actual household

1. **Contacts and communication:** Use [Ready.gov's planning route](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery) to choose a trusted out-of-area contact, one nearby and one farther meeting point, and how children or others away from home will reconnect. Write numbers on a private paper copy in case a phone is lost or uncharged. Ask a school, workplace, building manager, or caregiver about their own reunification process rather than assuming everyone will be home.
2. **Find local alerts:** Check the [state emergency management directory](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery), then find your city, county, tribal, or territorial emergency management office's official alerts and phone route. Review [Ready.gov's emergency-alert explanation](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery). Wireless Emergency Alerts may arrive on capable phones without signing up, while many local text or email systems require enrollment. As another channel, check [NOAA Weather Radio county coverage](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery); a suitable receiver and reliable local reception are needed. Ask about language and accessible formats, and use more than one channel if possible.
3. **Evacuation or shelter:** Discuss routes, transport, an accessible destination, a backup if the first place is unavailable, and how to learn whether a particular shelter is open. Check [Ready.gov's evacuation and shelter guidance](https://www.ready.gov/evacuation) and [shelter overview](https://www.ready.gov/shelter) beforehand. Ask the local office about transport without a car, mobility support, service animals or pets, and communication assistance. A map or a prior shelter listing is not a current opening or a safe route.
4. **Essentials and documents:** Build a kit gradually from [Ready.gov's kit guidance](https://www.ready.gov/kit) and the [short printable checklist](emergency-essentials-checklist.md). Pack for the people and animals in your care, the hazard, and a possible power or service outage. Keep private copies of identity, insurance, medical, and contact information protected; do not post them in a public checklist or shared repository.

**Adapt the plan with the people it affects.** For disability, mobility, sensory or communication needs, or powered medical equipment, [Ready.gov's disability planning](https://www.ready.gov/people-disabilities) recommends arranging accessible transport and power contingencies in advance. Ask the device supplier, clinician, pharmacy, utility, and local emergency office about your specific backup needs; do not improvise medical power or treatment. Include children, older adults, caregivers, languages and interpreters, and [pets](https://www.ready.gov/pets). Confirm each destination's actual access and animal policy. A support contact or registry does not guarantee a rescue or a shelter place.

## During: current instructions outrank this page

- Check the official local alerts and another usable channel, such as radio, a verified agency phone line, or a trusted in-person information point if the network is down. A weather forecast is not an evacuation order, and a national directory cannot confirm an open local shelter.
- Follow the instructions for *your area and incident* from the responsible local officials. Help household members communicate the plan within their capacity; do not take an unsafe route to retrieve supplies or documents.
- If displaced and safe enough to seek help, ask the local emergency office or [211 and urgent service gateways](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance) for a current shelter and accessible transport route. Confirm location, hours, household/pet accommodations, and whether a call or referral is required. 211 is a referral, not emergency dispatch or a reservation.

## After: stabilize, document, then apply

1. **Wait for safe access:** Follow official re-entry and utility instructions. Do not enter a damaged or hazardous property to photograph it, retrieve documents, or start repairs. Seek urgent medical help for an acute concern rather than starting paperwork.
2. **Record only what is safe:** When access is permitted, take dated photos or notes of damage, lost possessions, and needed temporary expenses; keep receipts and any notices. Save them privately. [Homeowners and flood insurance guidance](../lists/housing-home-and-utilities/README.md#homeowners-and-flood-insurance) explains claim numbers, inspections, coverage questions, and what to ask before permanent repairs. Renters can ask their landlord and insurer about their own responsibilities and coverage.
3. **Find the correct assistance:** The [USA.gov disaster-assistance guide](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery) points to the federal area lookup, application routes, and options when there is no federal declaration. Confirm the **event, designated area, Individual Assistance status, registration window, and your circumstances** on the current official record. A declaration alone does not make every household or every program eligible. Existing [DisasterAssistance.gov and recovery-center routes](../lists/awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#disaster-and-safety-support) handle applications and in-person help. USA.gov also lists phone application at **1-800-621-3362** (checked September 23, 2026); verify the current number when possible. Do not wait for a federal decision to ask about urgent local help.
4. **Keep tracks separate:** Use [Financial Relief and Assistance](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#local-and-disaster-assistance) for benefits and hardship; [Housing, Home, and Utilities](../lists/housing-home-and-utilities/README.md#start-here) for temporary housing, repairs, and insurance; and [disaster legal help](../lists/legal-help-and-law/README.md#disaster-recovery-and-legal-help) for appeals, rights, and deadlines. Save each claim or application confirmation and ask each program about its own evidence and follow-up date. An insurance claim, aid application, contractor agreement, and legal appeal are different actions.
5. **Screen offers and unmet needs:** [FTC disaster-scam guidance](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#emergency-preparedness-and-disaster-recovery) covers impersonators, rushed repairs, upfront payment, and false fees for FEMA help. Verify the agency or contractor independently and keep contracts in writing. For food, shelter, medicines, or utilities that cannot wait, use [211](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance), the local emergency office, and existing [urgent assistance paths](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#start-here); [mutual-aid groups](../lists/awesome-abundance/community-sharing-and-material-resources/README.md#gifting-and-reuse-networks) may be additional leads, with capacity and safety checked directly.

**For an actual incident:** On your private copy, note the event name/date, the official declaration or agency page you checked, your exact area and available program, the deadline shown, and the date you verified each fact. Recheck after a change in instructions, location, designation, or notice. Do not publish addresses, medical information, photos of private property, claim numbers, or household contact lists.
